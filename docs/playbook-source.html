Read this first

How to read this playbook

Nobody outside the AI companies knows exactly how they pick stores. So every claim here is labelled with how much we can trust it.

Official stated by the AI company itself  Research peer-reviewed study  Industry data large published datasets  Reported official claim we could only verify second-hand  Observed small-scale tracking, indicative only
Rule for the system: advice backed only by "Observed" must be worded as a likely reason, never as a fact. Official and Research can be stated plainly.

How an AI shopping answer is made

Roughly the same for Claude, ChatGPT, Google and Perplexity.

1. The questionA shopper asks in their own words. ChatGPT may also use their memory and custom instructions. Reported
2. It searchesThe AI writes its own search terms, often several at once ("query fan-out" in Google's words). Official
3. It readsWeb pages from its search index, plus product data from feeds (Shopify Catalog, Merchant Center, merchant feeds). Official
4. It picksWeighs price, reviews, availability and fit to the request. The weighting changes with intent: a "£30 budget" makes price matter more. Reported
5. It names sellersChatGPT ranks merchants on availability, price, quality and whether they're the maker or primary seller. Reported
The two ways a store wins. (a) The AI reads and trusts the store's own pages and product data, or (b) the AI reads other websites that recommend the store (reviews, Reddit, gift guides, press). Our checks tell us which one is failing. That is the core of every diagnosis.
Paid placement doesn't buy organic answers. OpenAI says ChatGPT product results aren't ads and aren't influenced by partnerships Reported. Google says ad spend doesn't affect AI Mode placement Reported. That's why small stores can genuinely compete.

The factors

What AI looks for: the 13 factors

Each factor has an ID used by the system (e.g. "F3"), the evidence, what we check, and the typical fix.

F1

Let AI's search robots in

Evidence

  • Sites that block OAI-SearchBot "will not be shown in ChatGPT search answers". GPTBot is only for training. Official
  • Blocking Claude-SearchBot "may reduce your site's visibility and accuracy" in Claude search. Blocking Claude-User limits pages fetched for a user's question. ClaudeBot is training only. Official
  • Google's AI features use the normal Search index. Pages must be indexed and eligible for snippets. Official
  • OpenAI's shopping research may skip retailers that block automated access and use similar sites instead. Reported

We check

robots.txt rules for every AI search robot; noindex and nosnippet tags; firewalls that block bots.

Typical fix: allow OAI-SearchBot, Claude-SearchBot, Claude-User, PerplexityBot and Googlebot. Blocking training-only robots (GPTBot, ClaudeBot, Google-Extended) is a free choice.
F2

Make the words readable by machines

Evidence

  • US retail product pages average only 66% AI readability. Homepages average 75% and FAQ pages 80%. Industry data
  • Google recommends crawlable pages, semantic HTML and good JavaScript practice for its AI features. Official

We check

Whether key info (description, price, delivery, sizes, reviews) is in the page text, or hidden in images, tabs that load later, or scripts.

Typical fix: put delivery, sizing and key details as plain text on the page. Turn image size charts into text.
F3

Provide clean, consistent product data

Evidence

  • ChatGPT uses "structured metadata from first-party and third-party providers (e.g. price, product description)". Reported
  • Shopify product data is integrated into ChatGPT through Shopify Catalog. Shopify's agentic storefronts channel is on by default for eligible stores. Reported
  • Google: Merchant Center feeds "can help" products appear in AI responses. Prices must match across feed, page, markup and checkout. Official
  • Structured data (schema) isn't required for Google's AI search, but it's required for merchant listing results and still recommended. Official

We check

Product schema (name, price, currency, availability, brand, reviews); whether the platform feeds exist; price consistency.

Typical fix: complete product schema; set up Google Merchant Center free listings; keep prices and stock in sync everywhere.
F4

Clear, current price and stock

Evidence

  • ChatGPT ranks merchants partly on availability and price. Reported
  • A tracked UK answer dropped a product because the maker's site showed it as out of stock. Observed

We check

Visible prices; stock status in text and data; mismatches between product data and page.

Typical fix: show price and stock clearly on every product. Mark sold-out items properly instead of leaving them looking available.
F5

Say you're the maker

Evidence

  • ChatGPT considers "whether the seller makes or primarily sells the product" when choosing which merchant to show. Reported

We check

Whether the store clearly says it designs, makes or is the official seller of its products, on the product pages, the About page and in the data (brand).

Typical fix: "Hand-poured by us in Lancashire" on every product, and the brand field set to the store's own name. A big advantage for small makers.
F6

Specific facts beat vague praise

Evidence

  • Adding statistics, quotations and cited sources raised a page's visibility in AI answers by up to ~40%. Keyword stuffing didn't help. Research
  • Lower-ranked sites gained most: citing sources lifted the 5th-ranked site's visibility by 115%, while the top site fell 30%. Research
  • Google favours "non-commodity" content with unique insight over generic text. Official

We check

Whether descriptions contain measurable facts (sizes, weights, burn time, materials, origin) or only adjectives ("beautiful", "luxury").

Typical fix: swap adjectives for facts: "50-hour burn, 220g soy wax, made in Lytham" beats "luxurious long-lasting candle".
F7

Answer the questions shoppers ask

Evidence

  • ChatGPT weighs price, reviews, availability and ease of use depending on what's asked. Reported
  • Google: no need to split content into chunks or chase every long-tail phrase. Its systems understand meaning, so write naturally. Official
  • The most-cited shop pages in tracked UK answers mostly showed price and stock; product pages were cited most. Observed

We check

Delivery cost and speed, returns, sizing or fit, who it's for, and care, on product pages, not just at checkout.

Typical fix: a short "Delivery and returns" line plus 3–5 FAQs on each product or category page.
F8

Reviews AI can see

Evidence

  • Perplexity checks product claims against independent third-party reviews before recommending. Reported
  • Google's Shopping Graph stores reviews alongside product details. Official
  • Reviews appear to fill the gap when the AI can't read a shop's own page. Observed

We check

Whether reviews are in the page text and the product data, and whether the store has reviews on independent sites (Google, Trustpilot and so on).

Typical fix: use a reviews app that adds rating data to product pages; collect Google and independent reviews.
F9

Be talked about on sites AI reads

Evidence

  • Across 680 million citations: Reddit made up 46.7% of Perplexity's top-10 cited sources and 21% of Google AI Overviews'. ChatGPT leans heavily on Wikipedia (47.9% of its top 10). Industry data
  • Google: chasing fake or "inauthentic" mentions doesn't help; its systems focus on quality and block spam. Official

We check

Which sites the AI read for the store's searches (from our checks), and whether the store or its rivals appear on them.

Typical fix: earn genuine mentions in gift guides, local press and maker directories, and get customers to post honestly in communities. Never fake posts.
F10

A clear story and identity

Evidence

  • ChatGPT also uses what the model already knows before it searches ("model responses generated… before it considers any new search results"), so being known matters. Reported
  • Google recommends a Google Business Profile for local businesses. Official

We check

An About page; consistent business name everywhere; Organisation data on the site; Google Business Profile (if they have local customers).

Typical fix: a proper About page (who, where, since when, what makes you different) and the same name used everywhere.
F11

Join the free shopping programmes

Evidence

  • Google: Merchant Center free listings feed the Shopping Graph used by AI Mode. Official
  • ChatGPT: merchants can apply to send a product feed (Agentic Commerce Protocol). Applying doesn't guarantee selection. Reported
  • Perplexity: a free merchant programme that accepts Google-style feeds. Reported

We check

Platform (Shopify Catalog on by default where eligible); signs of a Merchant Center feed.

Typical fix: set up Google Merchant Center free listings first (biggest reach), then the others if eligible.
F12

Match the shopper's intent

Evidence

  • The weighting of factors changes with the request: an explicit budget makes price matter more. Reported

We check

Whether the store has pages that match common intents: gifts, budgets, occasions, "for him/her", values (vegan, eco).

Typical fix: collection pages like "Gifts under £30" or "Vegan candles", with clear prices and a short intro.
F13

Fresh, up-to-date information

Evidence

  • OpenAI says shopping research reads retailer and public site information in real time. Stale prices or stock work against you. Reported
  • Our checks record source dates. Markets where AI relies on old articles are open to fresh content. Observed

We check

Dates of the sources the AI used; old policy pages still live; out-of-date prices.

Typical fix: remove or redirect outdated pages (old returns policies); refresh key pages each season.

Myths: things that don't (currently) matter

MythWhat the evidence says
"You need an llms.txt file"Google says you don't need special AI files or Markdown versions to appear in its AI search. Official
"Special AI schema or markup"Not required for Google's generative AI search. Normal product schema is still useful (F3). Official
"Chunk your content for AI"Google's systems understand several topics on one page. No need to split it up. Official
"Stuff in keywords"Keyword stuffing performed below baseline in the GEO study. Research
"Pay for ads to appear in AI answers"ChatGPT product results aren't ads Reported; Google ad spend doesn't affect AI Mode placement Reported. Ads are separate, labelled placements.
"Buy mentions or fake reviews"Google says inauthentic mentions don't help, and its systems block spam Official. Fake reviews are also illegal in the UK.

For the build

How the system uses this playbook

1. Every fix must cite a factor and evidence. Example: "F7 · AI picked Wick & Wild 4 times saying 'free next-day delivery'. Your delivery info only appears at checkout." No factor plus evidence means no advice.
2. Confidence shapes the wording. Official and Research: stated plainly. Reported: "according to OpenAI…". Observed: "likely" or "may".
3. The AI-ready checklist (stage 7) is built from F1–F13, using only the things we can check directly on the store.
4. Our own data grows the playbook. As checks build up, we add Observed patterns from our own results, e.g. "in candle stores, stores with visible delivery info were picked X% more". Over time that becomes knowledge nobody else has.
5. Review every 3 months. AI shopping changes fast: OpenAI retired its Instant Checkout button in March 2026, and Google's Universal Commerce Protocol is still in early access. Next review: February 2027.
